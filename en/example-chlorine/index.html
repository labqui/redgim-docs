Free chlorine in tap water

This is the most complete worked example REDGIM has: a real determination, compared against the reference instrumental method, with the successes and the failures on display. It works as a recipe and as a warning.

Why chlorine​

Free chlorine is the disinfectant in drinking water, and Brazilian regulation requires a minimum of 0.2 mg/L anywhere in the distribution network, with a 5 mg/L ceiling Ministério da Saúde (Brasil). Portaria nº 518 de 25/03/2004. 2006.. It is a measurement that has to be made often, at many points, and away from the laboratory. That is exactly the kind of problem where a phone app competes with an instrument.

The method, in short​

The reagent is o-tolidine, which in the presence of free chlorine and in acidic medium oxidises into a yellow compound Johnson, J. D.; Overby, R. Stabilized Neutral Orthotolidine, SNORT, Colorimetric Method for Chlorine. Analytical Chemistry v. 41, n. 13, p. 1744-1750, 1969. doi:10.1021/ac60282a027. The more chlorine, the deeper the yellow.

Reagento-tolidine solution, sold in pool supply shops
Working range0.160 to 0.800 mg/L
Curvecalibration blank, 5 standards and the samples
Total timeunder 30 minutes, preparation included
Approximate costaround R$ 20 for 14 complete analytical curves

Cost and time are what make this example viable in a classroom: polystyrene cuvettes cost less than a real each and are reusable.

The colour does not wait

O-tolidine solutions fade quickly, and above the working range the colour drifts from yellow to orange. Photograph right after preparing. That hurry is in fact an advantage of the image method: a single photo records the whole curve at once, while the spectrophotometric reading is done cuvette by cuvette.

The capture, and what it teaches​

The same curve was photographed two ways. The difference between them is the main lesson on this page.

In volumetric flasks​

From left to right: the calibration blank, five standards in increasing concentration and two tap water samples. The red markers are the regions of interest.

Notice where they are: on the neck, just below the meniscus. That is not an aesthetic choice. The bulb of the flask is curved and reflects ambient light, creating a bright band that has nothing to do with concentration. The neck is straight and narrow, and the colour there is even.

Even so, the flask imposes a limit: the regions fitted at 65 pixels a side, about 4,000 pixels each.

In polystyrene cuvettes​

The same solutions, transferred to cuvettes. The walls are straight, the vessels sit side by side taking up less than 9 cm in total, and the camera could get much closer.

The gain is direct: the regions went to 150 by 200 pixels, that is, 30,000 pixels each, seven times more than in the flask. Narrower distributions, a firmer measurement.

The validation​

The same standards and samples were read on a benchtop spectrophotometer at 433 nm, with univariate regression. REDGIM used PLS with 2 latent variables, optimised inside the app.

Both samples came out at 0.73 and 0.74 mg/L of free chlorine once the dilution was accounted for, inside the range the regulation requires. The image method would reach the same conclusion as the benchtop one.

The limitations, unvarnished​

The flask did not work. Against the reference, the relative error was 1.3% and 2.7% with cuvettes, against 8.2% and 16% with flasks. An error above 8% is evidence that the model, however much its R² reads 0.9999, has no predictive capability. It stands as a demonstration that a high R² does not rescue a poor capture.

The detection limits are worse than the instrument's, by an order of magnitude. For the range the regulation demands that does not get in the way, but the method does not replace the bench where trace level is required.

The app does not compute uncertainty. Uncertainty in multivariate analysis costs enough processing and memory to rule out too many devices. The reference values above carry uncertainty, computed with the Eurachem guide S L R Ellison; A Williams (Eds). Eurachem/CITAC guide: Quantifying Uncertainty in Analytical Measurement. English v. 3, p. 126, 2012.; REDGIM's do not.

Where to read the full method​

This application was published in a peer reviewed journal, and the article details the procedure, the data treatment and the figures of merit:

REDGIM as a smartphone app for chemometrics applications through image analysis: a use in PLS. Química Nova.

What to take from this example​

  1. The container decides more than the model. Swapping flasks for cuvettes improved the error sixfold, without changing a line of the calculation.
  2. Get the camera closer. Seven times more pixels in the region is what changed.
  3. Distrust R² on its own. Both models read 0.9999.
  4. Compare against a reference method at least once, before trusting a new routine.
