Figures of merit

A model returns a number for each sample. Figures of merit answer a different question, one that comes first: what that number is worth. They measure the error, the precision, the linearity and the limit below which the method stops telling the sample apart from nothing.

This page covers the ones REDGIM computes and shows. None of them is required to use the app; they exist for when the result has to be defended.

Why multivariate changes the arithmetic​

Some measures do not change when you move from univariate to multivariate: accuracy, precision, robustness, fit and systematic error are estimated the same way. Others change in nature: linearity, sensitivity, signal-to-noise ratio, selectivity and confidence intervals Patrícia Valderrama, J. W. B. B. e. R. J. P. ESTADO DA ARTE DE FIGURAS DE MÉRITO EM CALIBRAÇÃO MULTIVARIADA. Quimica Nova v. 32, n. 5, p. 1278-1287, 2009. doi:10.1590/S0100-40422009000500034.

The difference lies in what counts as the analyte's signal. In a univariate method, subtracting the blank already gives the net signal. In a multivariate method, the net analyte signal is the fraction of the signal that is orthogonal to the signal of everything else in the sample, that is, what is left after discounting the interferents' contribution.

That is why asking "what is the sensitivity in this channel" makes no sense: the answer lives in the combination of the three, and it is the model that decides the combination.

The error: PRESS, MSE and RMSE​

It all starts in the same place: the sum of the squared differences between the reference value and what the model predicted Filgueiras, P. R. Determinação da composição de blends de petróleos utilizando FTIR-ATR e calibração multivariada. Universidade Federal do Espírito Santo p. 113, 2011..

PRESS=∑i=1N(yi−y^i)2MSE=PRESSNPRESS = \sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i})^2 \qquad MSE = \frac{PRESS}{N}PRESS=i=1∑N​(yi​−y^​i​)2MSE=NPRESS​

PRESS sums, MSE averages. Neither is in the unit of the measured property, because both are squared. The root fixes that:

RMSE=PRESSνRMSE = \sqrt{\frac{PRESS}{\nu}}RMSE=νPRESS​​

An RMSE in ppm reads as ppm. It is the metric to look at first, because it answers in the same unit as the question: how much the model misses, on average, on the scale you work in.

RMSEC and RMSEP are not the same sum​

Both use the formula above and differ in ν\nuν, the degrees of freedom:

What it measuresν\nuν
RMSECerror over the standards, which the model has seenN−k−1N - k - 1N−k−1, with mean-centred data
RMSEPerror over prediction samples, which it has not seenthe number of prediction samples

Discounting kkk in RMSEC is not a detail: kkk is the number of factors in the model, and each extra factor consumes a degree of freedom. A model with too many factors learns the noise of its own standards and drives RMSEC down without having got any better, so a low RMSEC on its own proves nothing. Comparing the two is what exposes the excess.

Precision and bias: SEC, SEP and bias​

Bias is the mean signed error, that is, how much the model misses always in the same direction:

Bias=∑i=1N(yi−y^i)NBias = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left ( y_{i} - \hat{y}_{i} \right )}{N}Bias=N∑i=1N​(yi​−y^​i​)​

A bias different from zero means the measuring condition has shifted: different lighting, a different reagent batch, a different camera. It is not scatter, it is displacement.

Take the bias out of the error and what remains is pure scatter, which is the measure of precision Forina, M.; Lanteri, S.; Casale, M. Multivariate calibration. Journal of Chromatography A v. 1158, n. 1-2, p. 61-93, 2007. doi:10.1016/j.chroma.2007.03.082:

SE=∑i=1N(yi−y^i−Bias)2N−1SE = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left ( y_{i}- \hat{y}_{i} - Bias \right ) ^2}{N-1}}SE=N−1∑i=1N​(yi​−y^​i​−Bias)2​​

The same pair as before applies: SEC for the calibration set, SEP for the prediction set.

Linearity: the coefficient of determination​

R2=1−∑i=1N(yi−y^i)2∑i=1N(yi−yˉ)2R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left ( y_{i} - \hat{y}_{i} \right )^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left ( y_{i} - \bar{y}\right )^2}R2=1−∑i=1N​(yi​−yˉ​)2∑i=1N​(yi​−y^​i​)2​

The closer to 1, the stronger the relationship. With the caveat this manual keeps repeating, and which you can produce with your own hands in Prediction Mode charts: R2R^2R2 cannot see the shape of the error. A clear curvature in the residuals lives happily with an R2R^2R2 of 0.99.

LOD and LOQ, and why they are a range​

The limit of detection is the smallest amount the method tells apart from the blank; the limit of quantification, the smallest it measures with confidence. REDGIM computes them by Olivieri's method, consistent with IUPAC Olivieri, A. C. Analytical Figures of Merit: From Univariate to Multiway Calibration. Chemical Reviews v. 114, n. 10, p. 5358-5378, 2014. doi:10.1021/ac501786u, with the usual factors: 3.33.33.3 for LOD and 101010 for LOQ.

Two things surprise anyone coming from univariate work.

Sensitivity is the inverse of the norm of the regression vector, 1/∥b∥1/\lVert b \rVert1/∥b∥. There is no line slope to call sensitivity: there is a vector, and its length is what says how much the signal responds to the property.

The limit is not a number, it is an interval. It depends on each sample's leverage, which measures how extreme that sample is within the model, and leverage changes from sample to sample. That is why the app shows a minimum and a maximum LOD: the best case, for a sample at the centre of the model, and the worst, for one at the edges.

Reading only the minimum is optimism. The whole range is the honest answer.

Where this shows up​

The model metrics sit in the prediction result tab, next to the charts that make them readable. How to read each chart is in Prediction Mode charts.
