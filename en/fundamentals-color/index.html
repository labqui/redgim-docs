Colour, light and the digital image

This page is not needed to use REDGIM. It is here for when you want to know why measuring colour with a camera works at all, and where the limits of that are.

Where this page applies

Colorimetry underpins one of the app's two paths, the prediction one, where colour is tied to a quantity.

Comparative mode depends on none of what is here. It does not ask what the colour means, nor does it need it to mean anything: it compares one region's distribution of intensities against the others and shows which ones group together. A sample may be coloured for any reason at all, and the comparison still holds.

The two paths share only the capture. From the histogram onwards, they do not talk to each other.

Colour is perception, not a measurement​

Colour is not in the object. It is what about six million cells in our eyes report about the light that reaches them, which is why there is no absolute reference for colour: each person sees what their own eyes deliver. The absence or deficiency of those cells causes colour blindness, named after a chemist who had the condition Lillo, J. A.; Álvaro, L.; Melnikova, A.; Moreira, H. Color Blindness. Reference Module in Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Psychology 2017. doi:10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.05503-6.

Hence the difficulty analytical chemistry faced for a century: turning colour into a number in a way that two laboratories arrive at the same value.

What the solution absorbs, and what it shows​

A molecule is coloured because it absorbs part of the light passing through it. What is left over, and reaches your eyes, is the complement of what was absorbed.

Violet400 - 435 nmYellow-green
Blue435 - 480 nmYellow

This is why a yellow solution responds in the blue channel: the yellow you see is what was left after the blue was absorbed. The more concentrated the solution, the more blue it holds back, and the darker the image's blue channel gets.

The law that holds colorimetry up​

The relationship between concentration and absorption is what makes colorimetry quantitative. It says absorbance grows in proportion to concentration Skoog, D. A.; West, D. M.; Holler, F. J.; Couch, S. Fundamentos de Química Analítica, Editora Thomson, tradução da 8ª edição. Cengage Learning. p. 1050, 2006.:

A=a⋅b⋅cA = a \cdot b \cdot cA=a⋅b⋅c

Where ccc is the concentration, bbb is the thickness the light travelled through and aaa is how strongly that particular substance swallows light. In practice aaa and bbb are held constant, and absorbance then varies only with how much substance is present. It is the basis of every spectrophotometer.

REDGIM does not measure absorbance

A spectrophotometer compares the light before and after it crosses the sample. The camera does not: it records the reflected light, without knowing how much went in.

The phenomenon is the same, observed from the other side. That is why the app does not report absorbance: it builds the relationship between the measured colour and the known values of your standards, and it is that relationship, not a physical law, that holds the estimate up.

Not just for solutions​

The law above talks about light crossing a liquid, and it is easy to conclude the method only serves solutions in a cuvette. It does not, and the reason is exactly the one above: since the app does not measure absorbance, it does not depend on there being an optical path either.

What it needs is a colour that varies with the quantity, and that happens in plenty of things that are not solutions:

  • a thin layer chromatography plate, with the spot developed;
  • a paper strip with a reaction spot, of the kind used in rapid tests;
  • a microplate well;
  • a powder, a tablet or a surface that changes colour with whatever you want to measure.

In those cases there is no liquid thickness and no transmission: what is measured is the colour reflected by the surface. The relationship with the quantity stops having a law behind it and becomes entirely empirical, built from your standards. In the app's practice, the procedure is the same. What changes is what you can claim about it.

The trade: three channels instead of a spectrum​

A spectrophotometer delivers hundreds of points along the spectrum. A camera delivers three, broad and overlapping: red, green and blue.

That is the same family of sensor found in the detectors of modern spectrophotometers Shishkin, Y. L.; Dmitrienko, S. G.; Medvedeva, O. M.; Badakova, S. A.; Pyatkova, L. N. Use of a Scanner and Digital Image-Processing Software for the Quantification of Adsorbed Substances. Journal of Analytical Chemistry v. 59, n. 2, p. 102-106, 2004. doi:10.1023/B:JANC.0000014733.32082.4b. You gain cost, portability and speed. You lose selectivity: if a coloured interferent is present, it falls inside the same three channels and there is no way to separate it by wavelength.

That is where the two requirements running through this whole manual come from: steady lighting and a white reference. They are what holds the comparison together, since there is no reference beam as in benchtop equipment.

What a pixel is​

A digital image is a grid of dots. Each dot, the pixel, holds three numbers from 0 to 255: how much red, how much green, how much blue. The combinations give around 16 million colours Gomes, M. S.; Trevizan, L. C.; Nóbrega, J. A.; Kamogawa, M. Y. Uso de scanner em espectrofotometria de absorção molecular: aplicação em experimento didático enfocando a determinação de ácido ascórbico. Quim. Nova v. 31, n. 6, p. 1577-1581, 2008..

When the chemical reaction leaves the sample darker, pinker or yellower, those three numbers change along with the amount of substance. That is exactly what REDGIM takes advantage of.
