PLS — Partial Least Squares & Latent Variables

Multivariate Calibration in Prediction Mode

This chapter details the mathematical foundations of Partial Least Squares Regression (PLS) in REDGIM. The demonstration is grounded in an authentic analytical experiment: the quantification of free chlorine (HClO\text{HClO}HClO) with ortho-tolidine in polystyrene cuvettes, illustrating the classic concept of rotating the optical space to discover the Latent Variable.

1. What is PLS and Why Does It Outperform Univariate Models?​

In conventional instrumental colorimetry (Beer-Lambert Law), absorbance is measured at a single fixed wavelength (A=ϵ⋅b⋅cA = \epsilon \cdot b \cdot cA=ϵ⋅b⋅c). In digital images, this corresponds to using only one color channel (e.g. only the blue channel).

However, smartphone cameras capture three broad, overlapping spectral channels (Red, Green, and Blue) simultaneously. Moreover, ambient light fluctuations, wall reflections, and optical scattering affect all channels together.

PLS (Partial Least Squares) is the chemometric technique that overcomes this constraint:

PLS constructs new synthetic variables (called Latent Variables) that combine multiple image color channels to maximize direct correlation with the chemical concentration (yyy).

2. The Crucial Difference: PCA versus PLS​

  • In PCA: The algorithm finds the direction of greatest variance in XXX without knowing yyy. If illumination noise dominates, PCA points towards the noise, not the chemistry!
  • In PLS: The algorithm rotates the axis to maximize the squared covariance between XXX and yyy: max⁡wCov⁡2(Xw,y)=max⁡w[Var⁡(Xw)⋅Corr⁡2(Xw,y)]\max_{w} \operatorname{Cov}^2(Xw, y) = \max_{w} \Big[ \operatorname{Var}(Xw) \cdot \operatorname{Corr}^2(Xw, y) \Big]wmax​Cov2(Xw,y)=wmax​[Var(Xw)⋅Corr2(Xw,y)] PLS filters out non-chemical variations and focuses squarely on analytical signal.

3. The 3D Space (X₁, X₂, Y) and the Line of Maximum Information​

To truly understand PLS, we visualize the dataset in a three-dimensional space (X1,X2,Y)(X_1, X_2, Y)(X1​,X2​,Y):

  • The floor (horizontal plane) contains the instrumental predictor variables: Green Channel (X1=ΔGX_1 = \Delta GX1​=ΔG) and Blue Channel (X2=ΔBX_2 = \Delta BX2​=ΔB);
  • The vertical axis (height) represents the chemical response to quantify: Free Chlorine Concentration (Y=[HClO]Y = [\text{HClO}]Y=[HClO] in mg/L);
  • Each cuvette analyzed is a floating point inside this 3D volume.

Why doesn't PCA solve calibration?​

When running PCA, the algorithm only evaluates the floor (X1,X2)(X_1, X_2)(X1​,X2​), finding the direction of highest variance in the camera signals. PCA is completely blind to the vertical height YYY. If optical noise (e.g., cuvette wall reflection or lamp fluctuation) perturbs the green channel, PCA tilts toward that noise, corrupting the chemical calibration.

What does PLS do in 3D space?​

PLS finds the 3D line passing through the point cloud that maximizes the mutual covariance between X and Y:

r(t)=(Xˉ1+t⋅w1,Xˉ2+t⋅w2,Yˉ+q⋅t)\mathbf{r}(t) = \Big(\bar{X}_1 + t \cdot w_1, \quad \bar{X}_2 + t \cdot w_2, \quad \bar{Y} + q \cdot t\Big)r(t)=(Xˉ1​+t⋅w1​,Xˉ2​+t⋅w2​,Yˉ+q⋅t)

As the line rotates in space:

  • In misaligned directions or at the blind angle (150∘150^\circ150∘), the line misses the data cloud, producing large 3D residuals and zero covariance;
  • When reaching the Latent Variable LV1 (θ≈64.1∘\theta \approx 64.1^\circθ≈64.1∘), the 3D line pierces the center of all 3D points simultaneously, minimizing orthogonal distances and capturing 100% of mutual information (R2=0.9999R^2 = 0.9999R2=0.9999).

5. The Real Experiment: Free Chlorine with Ortho-Tolidine​

HClO+ortho-tolidine→acidyellow quinoid compound\text{HClO} + \text{ortho-tolidine} \xrightarrow{\text{acid}} \text{yellow quinoid compound}HClO+ortho-tolidineacid​yellow quinoid compound

As free chlorine concentration rises:

  • The solution becomes increasingly yellow;
  • Yellow absorbs blue light (≈433 nm\approx 433\text{ nm}≈433 nm), driving strong attenuation in the Blue Channel (ΔB\Delta BΔB);
  • The Green Channel (ΔG\Delta GΔG) exhibits moderate attenuation;
  • The Red Channel (ΔR\Delta RΔR) remains mostly transparent.

w1=Xc⊤yc∥Xc⊤yc∥≈[0.4360.900]w_1 = \frac{X_c^{\top} y_c}{\|X_c^{\top} y_c\|} \approx \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0.436} \\ \mathbf{0.900} \end{bmatrix}w1​=∥Xc⊤​yc​∥Xc⊤​yc​​≈[0.4360.900​] θPLS=arctan⁡(wBwG)≈64.1∘\theta_{PLS} = \arctan\left(\frac{w_B}{w_G}\right) \approx \mathbf{64.1^\circ}θPLS​=arctan(wG​wB​​)≈64.1∘
